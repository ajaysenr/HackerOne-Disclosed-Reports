## Vulnerability Details

Rockstar Games does not validate the ownership of an email before allowing access on its support portal . This leads to "ticket trick attack". An attacker can login, signup, and interact as "support@rockstargames.com" .
Steps to reproduce :
1. Lets say Rockstar games has an account on website "organisation.com" with email "support@rockstargames.com".
2. the website "organisation.com" sends a login link when an email is entered on the login page.
3. The login link is sent from email address "support@organisation.com"
4. An attacker creates an account on Rockstar Games with email "support@organisation.com"
5. Attacker logs into Rockstar Games support portal.
6. Attacker goes to "organisation.com" and requests a login link for " support@rockstargames.com" .
7. An email containing login link is sent to "support@rockstargames.com" from "support@organisation.com"
8.This creates a support ticket for "support@organisation.com".
9. Since the attacker is logged in as "support@organisation.com" on "Rockstar Games' support portal he/she can see the contents of the email in "Dashboard" on support portal .
10. The attacker opens the login link and logs in as " support@rockstargames.com" on "organisation.com". 

To demonstrate impact, I have created an account on workspace management site "range.co" , the login is done through a link sent on the email but I was able to login as support@rockstargames.com without owning that email address through exploitation of this vulnerability.

## Impact

An attacker can login/reset passwords for accounts belonging to support@rockstargames.com on various site leading to unauthorised access and sensitive information disclosure.
